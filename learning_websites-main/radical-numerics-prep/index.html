Radical Numerics — technical brief

An AI research lab building general biological intelligence: one multimodal model that reads, interprets, and writes biology across DNA, RNA, proteins, epigenomics, and tokenized protein structure. The flagship is Omnii-1, spanning 28 biological modalities. The team invented the architectures underneath it — Hyena, HyenaDNA, Evo, Evo 2 — and the role you are interviewing for owns the layer that turns that base model into something scientifically useful.

Know the pivot — the single most useful piece of context you can bring

Radical Numerics was announced in August 2025 with a different pitch. Poli's launch post described "the engine for recursive self-improvement (RSI): AI that designs and refines AI," built on three claims: the world needs orders of magnitude more purpose-built AI systems, human development speed is the bottleneck, and frontier AI development is ripe for disruption. In October 2025 they shipped RND1, a 30B diffusion language model for general text.

By June 2026 they launched publicly with $50M and a different framing entirely: general biological intelligence with a dual mandate on biodefense. The through-line is not the domain, it is the method — architecture invention plus large-scale training, pointed at whichever domain has the most headroom. Do not describe them as a diffusion-LM company. Do notice that an automated-architecture-design instinct runs through the founding team, and that RND1's any-order parallel generation is a live asset for a model whose job is to write sequences into existing context.

The thesis, in their words

"We are beginning to see a fundamental shift in biological AI: generalist models can now outperform specialized models on tasks they were individually designed to solve. This echoes the transformation in natural language AI, where general-purpose LLMs trained at scale began surpassing systems engineered for narrow tasks." The corollary they state explicitly: "the ultimate measure of a general biological model is how broadly the model can be applied, and how much of what it learns can transfer to new problems." Transfer is the product. That is why eight applications shipped on one day.

Part 2

Research lineage — the architectures this team invented

Unusually for a startup, almost every architectural component in their stack has their names on it. Know this chain; it is the substance of "we built new architectures when existing approaches fell short."

2.1 Hyena — subquadratic attention replacement

The foundational piece, by Poli, Massaroli, Ré and colleagues. Attention costs O(L²) in sequence length, which caps context. Hyena replaces it with an interleaving of two cheap operations: implicitly parameterized long convolutions and data-controlled gating.

// Hyena operator, order-2 form (x₁, x₂, v) ↦ H(x₁, x₂) v where H(x₁, x₂) = D_x₂ · T_h · D_x₁ D_x = diagonal matrix from a projection of the input → "gating", data-controlled T_h = Toeplitz matrix of a learned long filter h → "mixing", content-agnostic // Two tricks make it cheap h is produced by a small MLP from positional encodings → filter length decoupled from params the convolution is evaluated by FFT → O(L log L) instead of O(L²)

The conceptual split matters: attention mixes tokens in a content-dependent way; Hyena separates that into a fixed long-range mixing operator plus input-dependent gates. Reported results: matches Transformer quality on The Pile and WikiText-103 with 20% less training compute at length 2k, 2× faster than optimized attention at 8k, 100× faster at 64k.

2.2 HyenaDNA — why DNA needs this specifically

Two problems with Transformers on genomes. First, context: quadratic attention limited prior genomic models to 512–4k tokens, under 0.001% of the human genome, making long-range regulation invisible. Second, and subtler, tokenization: k-mer and BPE tokenizers aggregate nucleotides into chunks, destroying single-nucleotide resolution — and a single-nucleotide polymorphism can abolish a protein's function. HyenaDNA uses a four-letter vocabulary, one nucleotide per token, a 1M-token context, trains up to 160× faster than Transformers at that length, and has global receptive field at every layer.

The point to carry forward

Byte-level tokenization plus subquadratic mixing is the enabling combination for genomics, and it is the opposite of the LLM trend toward larger vocabularies. If asked why genomics models look different from text models: the unit of meaning is one character, and the span of meaning is a million of them.

2.3 Evo and Evo 2 — generative genomics

Evo — Science, 2024
  • 7B parameters, 131,072-token context, StripedHyena.
  • ~300B nucleotides, prokaryotic genomes and phage.
  • Prediction and generation across DNA, RNA and protein — the central dogma in one model.
  • Generated EvoCas9-1, a functional CRISPR-Cas9 system unknown in nature, after testing just eleven designs. ~73% sequence identity to standard SpCas9 with comparable activity.
  • Later used by external researchers to generate the first complete AI-designed genome, a bacteriophage.
Evo 2 — Nature, 2026
  • 1B / 7B / 40B parameters. The 40B has 50 layers, hidden 8192, FFN 22528, 64 heads.
  • 9.3T tokens from 15,032 eukaryotic and 113,379 prokaryotic genomes. Trained on 2,048 H100s.
  • Context extended to 1,048,576 tokens, with verified needle-in-a-haystack recall.
  • Two-phase schedule: pretrain at 1024 context for 6.6T tokens, extend to 8192 for 1.1T, then mid-train to 1M context via positional interpolation plus RoPE base-frequency increase, with the data mix shifted toward whole genomes.
  • Largest fully open-source AI project in any domain, per the company.

2.4 RND1 — the detour that is still relevant

October 2025. RND1-Base is a 30.5B-parameter sparse mixture-of-experts diffusion language model with 3.3B active parameters per token, the largest open DLM at the time. It was not trained from scratch: it was converted from Qwen3-30B-A3B, an autoregressive model, by continual pretraining on 500B tokens. MMLU 69.6%, GSM8K 80.0%, MBPP 65.4% — state of the art among open DLMs.

The A2D (autoregressive-to-diffusion) recipe is the contribution, and it is a post-training result in all but name. Two problems, two deliberately simple answers:

ChallengePrior approachRND1's choice
Give the model bidirectional context when the base used causal attention Attention-mask annealing, with a transition policy and schedule to tune Replace the causal mask with a bidirectional mask at initialization. No schedule, no annealing hyperparameters.
Retain knowledge from trillions of tokens of AR pretraining Largely unaddressed in prior work Restrict updates to specific layers during conversion, preserving pretrained knowledge rather than overwriting it.
They also studied initialization, layer-specific learning rates, and critical batch size at scale — exactly the recipe science this role does.
Why bring this up in an interview

Diffusion language models generate in any order and in parallel, not strictly left-to-right. For a model whose job is to write DNA into an existing genomic context — Omnii's phage work explicitly "generates DNA using context on both sides of a selected design region" — bidirectional, any-order generation is not a curiosity, it is the natural objective. Connecting A2D to infilling-style genome design is a genuinely good observation and shows you read past the homepage.

Part 3

Omnii-1 — what it actually is

3.1 Three classes of model, and why Omnii is a third thing

The single most important technical distinction in this brief. Get it right and most of the benchmark story follows.

3.2 Tracks, and why the genome is the right unifying frame

Nearly every cell carries the same DNA; cell types differ in how that DNA is regulated. Those regulatory states are measurable — which chromatin is open, where transcription factors bind, which cytosines are methylated, which histone marks are deposited. Each assay, aligned back to the genome, yields a track: a signal defined at every genomic coordinate.

The architectural insight: every track shares the genome's coordinate system. That gives a common index for fusing heterogeneous modalities, which is normally the hard part of multimodal learning. Their framing: "the genome provides a natural foundation for this unification, serving as both a common reference for molecular biology and a record shaped by billions of years of evolution."

The attribution result worth quoting

For ORM2, a plasma protein made almost entirely in the liver, they attribute each predicted base of RNA-seq coverage back to the surrounding 12 kb. In liver, the strongest contributions come from the gene's own promoter, exon 2, and the last exon, with conservation, DNA, and Pol II occupancy carrying most of the influence. In skeletal muscle, H3K27ac, H3K4me3 and Pol II at the ORM2 promoter are at least an order of magnitude lower, and the model correctly predicts almost no coverage. The cleanest demonstration that the model uses biological context rather than memorizing the gene.

3.3 The two variant scores — know both by name

Omnii-1 exposes two distinct scoring modes, and the genetics work uses them for different jobs. This distinction will make you sound like you read the primary sources.

Omnii-1 DNA

Reads the sequence around a variant twice, once with the reference base and once with the alternate, and measures how much less the alternate fits its surrounding context. A strongly negative score means the change disrupts learned sequence patterns, much of which reflects evolutionary constraint.

Sequence-only, works anywhere in the genome, coding or non-coding. This is the gLM-style score.

Omnii-1 track

Predicts how the variant changes molecular signals in a chosen biosample — chromatin accessibility, histone marks, transcription, splicing — and combines them into one disruption score, while the individual tracks show which signals move and in which direction.

This is what lets the model propose a mechanism and a cell type, not just a severity.

Both are reported as percentiles against a matched gnomAD background: ranked separately for common variants (above 5% frequency) and rare ones (seen once), compared only against background variants from the same kind of region — coding, intronic, intergenic — and the same type of base change. Lower means more unusual. That matching is a careful piece of statistical hygiene worth noticing.

3.4 Pretraining, mid-training, post-training

They consistently name three stages, and the distinction is the organizing structure of the role.

Pretraining

Native multimodal training across all 28 modalities on trillions of tokens — DNA, RNA, proteins, epigenomics, and tokenized protein structure — learning a shared representation. Where base capability and scaling laws live.

Mid-training

The stage most ML people outside genomics have never run. In Evo 2 it meant extending context from 8k to 1M via positional interpolation and RoPE base-frequency scaling with a data mix shifted toward whole genomes. For Omnii it also covers aligning new modalities and new domains — the antibody work used continued mid-training before post-training.

Post-training

Task adaptation: scoring variants, ranking designs, predicting a brand-new assay, generating sequence under constraints. Every one of the eight applications is an instance. This is the job.

Part 5

The eight applications, in depth

Released together on 20 October 2026 (cancer vaccines came earlier, 8 September), each the same base model plus mid- or post-training. The company organizes them by which core capability they use.

5.1 Omnii Phage — the headline result

Write First AI-designed phages administered to a human patient, under a physician-led, government-regulated compassionate-use pathway. They decline to disclose the clinical course to protect privacy, and say plainly that one patient establishes neither safety nor efficacy.

The biology that makes it a hard design problem: a phage must attach to a compatible receptor on the bacterial surface, deliver its genome, survive bacterial defenses like restriction-modification and CRISPR-Cas, and replicate. Failure at any step kills the infection. So editing a phage to change which bacteria it recognizes risks breaking everything downstream.

Prioritization — the ranking result
  • Variants held out from predictor training were ranked by predicted fitness, then compared against the full measured library across three bacterial strains.
  • On two strains with broad activity ranges, 97 and 96 of the top 100 exceeded the activity threshold.
  • On a hard strain where only 4.5% of the library cleared the bar, 24% of the top 100 did — a 5× enrichment.
  • Top-100 lists shared 57 members across the two easy strains; the hard strain's top picks were disjoint. Strain-specific prioritization is doing real work.
  • The honest read: on the hard strain, three quarters of the model's most confident picks still failed.
Host prediction — generalization
  • Trained on public phage–host interaction data (Gaborieau, 32,681 E. coli interactions), using genomic contigs without species-specific engineered features.
  • On randomly held-out strains: competitive with Gaborieau's per-phage models and GenoPHI.
  • Holding out an entire 37-strain clade — because random holdouts "may have close relatives in the training data" — Omnii reached an active phage for 91.4% of strains with just two recommendations, beating both specialists.
  • Cross-species transfer: adding E. coli data improved P. aeruginosa average precision from 0.570 to 0.608, past GenoPHI's 0.581 — while GenoPHI got worse with the same extra data.
The cross-species result is the most interview-relevant line in this post

A specialist model degraded when given out-of-domain data; the generalist improved. That is the clearest single piece of evidence for their whole thesis, and it has a direct post-training reading: data mixture decisions that hurt a narrow model can help a broad one. If they ask why a general model is worth the cost, this is the example to reach for.

5.2 Omnii Genetics — three tiers of human genetics

ReadInterpret The most thorough of the posts, and the one that best shows their evaluation discipline. It spans the full genetic architecture of disease.

The RYR1 case — worth being able to tell as a story

A girl with congenital fiber type disproportion and her unaffected parents, all three with long-read and short-read whole-genome plus whole-exome sequencing. The search space was fixed from the diagnosis alone, before any variant was examined: the seven known CFTD genes. Thirty-four rare variants were called in the proband across those genes, ten well-supported. The compelling evidence landed in RYR1, which causes autosomal recessive CFTD — meaning you need a damaging variant on both inherited copies.

Their control design in the Parkinson's work — steal this

Before testing any predictor, they validated the whole pipeline with a positive and a negative control. Carriers of rare predicted-loss-of-function variants in 14 monogenic PD genes develop the disease 7.5 years earlier (p = 3 × 10⁻²⁴). Rare synonymous variants in the same genes, which should not disrupt function, show no association (p = 0.61).

Only then did they remove all pLoF variants and ask each predictor to rank the remaining 124,432 missense, synonymous and non-coding variants. A known pathogenic variant, PRKN p.R275W, was eventually selected by all six predictors — but Omnii reached it at rank 22, where Evo 2 needed 121, REVEL 257, Borzoi 333, AVI 351, and CADD 437. Having a built-in positive control that every method must eventually find, and measuring how fast each gets there, is a genuinely elegant evaluation design.

5.3 Omnii Diagnostics — the flagship post-training result

Read With ClearNote Health. Post-trained to predict cell-free 5-hydroxymethylcytosine (5hmC), an epigenetic mark entirely absent from pretraining. This is the case study to know cold, because it is the purest demonstration of what post-training buys.

They also do the impact arithmetic, carefully hedged

Roughly 110 million US adults aged 50–79, and historical stage I–II incidence implies about 733,000 annual early-stage diagnoses. A hypothetical one-percentage-point sensitivity gain would correspond to about 7,300 additional detections at full participation; applying a modeled 0.23 deaths averted per cancer intercepted gives roughly 1,700 deaths averted within five years, per percentage point. They label every step as illustrative. That combination — do the arithmetic, then flag it as a proxy — is a good model for how to talk about impact without overclaiming.

5.4 Omnii Defense — recognizing function, not resemblance

Read The dual mandate as a shipped product: a restricted-access API plus an edge deployment that runs on a laptop. The framing is sharp and worth internalizing because it generalizes far beyond biodefense.

Conventional screening pattern-matches a sequence against a reference database. But an engineered sequence can preserve its function while diverging from both the known wild-type sequence and its known structure. They call the resulting gap an attack surface: the space between recognizable homology and preserved biological function. Homology search fails there by construction.

Metagenomic short reads — the harder regime

~150 base pairs, with pathogen-to-background ratios around 1 in 1,000. Their analogy: parsing the intent of an op-ed given one sentence. They distilled a lightweight embedding, classification and retrieval model with a function-aware training recipe.

  • 24 viral species, mutated to span 60–100% identity to wild type; 1,000 simulated reads spiked into wastewater backgrounds.
  • Mean recall gain over the SecureBio MGS workflow: ~31% for arbitrary mutations, ~52% for synonymous.
  • False positive rate stays at or below 0.5% in every condition, averaging 0.23% at 1M background reads.
  • Embedding trajectories drift toward a shared region as divergence grows, largely independent of species — evidence the representation tracks mutation consistently.
The genus-ablation experiment

The most rigorous control in any of their posts. They removed each target's species and then its entire genus from the reference databases and retrained Omnii checkpoints with the matching exclusions — putting the world back in the state it was actually in before each virus was discovered.

  • Conventional MGS workflow recovered under 0.1% of read pairs.
  • Omnii recovered 44.6% for dengue and 22.0% for Bundibugyo.
  • Real-world motivation: in the 2026 DRC Ebola outbreak, initial clinical testing returned negative before Bundibugyo was identified — it was outside the assay's scope. "A test can work as designed and still miss a pathogen it was never configured to recognize."
Edge deployment, and why it is a real engineering claim

An Oxford Nanopore MinION sequencer paired with a MacBook Pro (M3 Max, 128 GB). Data streams from the sequencer through basecalling, bioinformatics, and Omnii simultaneously, with a local interface showing live sequencing progress, flagged reads, functional classifications and taxonomic summaries — plus a locally running chat model to help interpret. They also report that Omnii can name the source species across the viral tree of life from a single 150-nucleotide read. Distilling a frontier multimodal model down to laptop inference is squarely a post-training and systems problem.

5.5 Omnii Cancer Vaccine — end-to-end personalized design

Write Published earlier than the rest, on 8 September 2026, by Alexander Fields, Michael Poli and Eric Nguyen. The clearest statement of why they think a general model beats a stack of specialists, and the post where the line "the algorithm is not merely helping discover the medicine, it is part of the medicine" appears.

The best evaluation reasoning in any of their posts

"When designing a cancer vaccine, we don't need Omnii to be right about every peptide, we need it to be very right about the best ones. A tumor might give us hundreds of possible neoantigens, while an actual vaccine may contain only a few dozen. Imagine receiving 500 possible wanted posters but being allowed to hand the immune system only 10. You care much more about whether the best targets are ranked 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 than you do about which candidate was ranked 437."

So they evaluate top-5, top-10 and top-20 precision, and Omnii's advantage is largest when the slot count is smallest. Then they simulate the actual decision: 100 candidates, 5 vaccine slots, ~6% truly immunogenic. Expected immunogenic peptides selected — Omnii 1.3, BigMHC-IM 0.7, NetMHCpan-4.1-BA 0.6, PRIME2.1 0.6, random 0.3. Translating an AUROC into "how many of your five shots on goal land" is exactly the metric-design instinct this role screens for.

They also caveat it properly: public immunogenicity datasets are small and heterogeneous, different experiments measure responses differently, and some MHC alleles have far more data than others — so they evaluate per allele before averaging. And: "a benchmark does not represent a patient."

5.6 Omnii Antibody — lab-in-the-loop with Aptamino

Write The closest thing to a pure post-training case study, and the one with the most methodological detail. Partner is Aptamino, a biotech startup building engineered antibodies. Three rounds completed since mid-September 2026.

Crucially: antibody sequences were not a dedicated domain in Omnii's original pretraining mixture. So this is adaptation of a general biological model to a domain it barely saw, which is precisely the job description.

Notice the one-hot baseline — this is the detail that proves you read it

A plain one-hot encoding of the sequence scores 0.246 on binding potency against Omnii's 0.273, with overlapping confidence intervals. The authors include it anyway. That is admirable practice, and it is also the honest limit of the result: on this dataset, a frontier multimodal foundation model is only modestly ahead of counting amino acids. Bringing this up — framed as appreciation for the baseline rather than a gotcha — demonstrates exactly the "question whether the metrics reflect reality" instinct the posting asks for. A natural follow-up: what would it take for the gap to widen — more rounds, more partners, or a different probing method?

How they specialized the model

Continued mid-training and post-training on a three-part mixture:

  • General protein sequences — a subset of the original pretraining distribution, included to preserve broad protein knowledge and prevent catastrophic forgetting.
  • Public antibody repertoires — exposure to antibody-specific sequence patterns.
  • Proprietary partner designs — experimentally characterized binders with measurements.

The stated risk they are managing: antibody repertoires contain extensive redundancy, with many sequences differing at a handful of positions, so naive training risks memorizing families instead of generalizing.

The leakage control, and why it is severe here

Cluster-aware training and evaluation splits. They cluster antibody and designed-binder datasets by sequence similarity and hold entire clusters out.

The number that justifies it: the median design shares 94% identity with its nearest neighbor. A random split would put near-identical sequences on both sides and massively overstate generalization.

They also use cluster-level bootstrap confidence intervals — 327 designs grouped into 172 clusters — so the error bars reflect the effective sample size, not the nominal one.

A neat probing experiment worth knowing

To test whether Omnii learned real biochemistry rather than just retaining input information, they compared linear probes for five calculable properties — charge, hydrophobicity, aliphatic index, aromatic content, molecular weight — against 500 fictitious control properties built by randomly reassigning values across amino acids, preserving the mathematical structure while destroying biochemical meaning. Charge and hydrophobicity beat all 500 shuffled controls; aromatic and aliphatic content showed smaller but significant effects. The logic — random projections of the input would recover any composition-based property, so you need shuffled controls to isolate what training actually added — is a clean piece of experimental design.

5.7 Omnii Rare Earths — transfer with zero in-domain data

Write With Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory. The purest transfer result in the set: Omnii had no rare-earth binding data in pretraining at all.

The problem is a national-security supply-chain one. China supplied 91% of refined rare earths in 2024 and 94% of permanent magnets, then tightened export controls through 2025. Proteins can selectively bind individual lanthanides, offering separation without harsh acids or large energy input — but only if you can find or design proteins that discriminate between chemically near-identical ions.

Two results here that cut against easy narratives — bring them up

Structure models underperformed. AlphaFold2 scored 0.191, below the simplest sequence-similarity baseline of 0.198. SaProt reached 0.217. Boltz-2 scored 0.262 when co-folded with individual lanthanides but 0.278 using protein representations alone — so explicitly modeling the bound ion made it worse. The reason is in the biology: selectivity depends on binding free energy differences, hydration shell displacement, and hydrogen-bond networks outside the coordination sphere, and small sequence changes far from the binding site can shift selectivity without visibly changing structure.

Evo 2 scored worst of all pretrained models at 0.133 — their own predecessor, evaluated on nucleotide sequences, while Omnii used its amino-acid representations. A useful, honest reminder that the right representation for a task matters more than model lineage, and that this team publishes results that make their own prior work look bad.

Their stated hypothesis for what comes next is directly post-training-shaped: "we hypothesize that models better at predicting selectivity are also better at generating selective binders," and they plan to use LLNL's measurements to further train the model, "as we have done in projects such as antibody design using partner assay data." Generated candidates are already in experimental validation.

Part 6

Post-training — the role, in depth

The posting's framing: "the next generation of biological foundation models will require not only new and improved pretraining recipes, but also innovation on post-training: the work that turns a powerful base model into a system that is useful, steerable, robust, and scientifically productive."

6.1 How this regime differs from LLM post-training

6.2 The method surface, mapped to what they actually did

Rejection sampling / ranked selection The workhorse. Phage variant prioritization (5× enrichment on the hard strain), antibody candidate selection, neoantigen top-5 selection. Generate many, score with the model's own predictors, test the top.
Preference / reward modeling Implicit in the fitness predictors trained on campaign measurements. Lab campaigns naturally yield pairs — same strain, same assay, same batch, one variant better than another.
New-modality alignment The 5hmC result: a self-supervised objective on an assay absent from pretraining, reaching 0.854 correlation where the WGS baseline sits at −0.057.
Domain adaptation via data mixture The antibody three-part mixture — general protein to prevent forgetting, public repertoires for domain patterns, partner designs for the specific task.
Distillation The biodefense short-read model: distilled into a lightweight embedding, classification and retrieval model, deployable on a laptop.

Part 7

Evaluation — where this role is really tested

The posting asks for someone who will "question whether the metrics reflect reality," "distinguish between signal and noise," "separate real effects from artifacts, bugs, or benchmark overfitting," and "identify when existing benchmarks stop being informative and should be replaced." Four statements of the same priority. Make evaluation the centerpiece of your preparation.

7.1 Metrics in this domain

MetricUsed forWhat it hides
Pearson r on tracksTrack reconstruction, methylation β, 5hmC Dominated by easy high-signal regions. A model can score 0.96 on ATAC by nailing the obvious peaks and missing everything subtle.
AUROCQTL causality, presentation, immunogenicity Misleading under class imbalance. The caQTL panels are ~8% positive, which is why AUPRC appears alongside.
AUPRCEnhancer-to-gene, caQTL Uninterpretable without its chance baseline — 0.045 for the E2G benchmark. An AUPRC of 0.49 against 0.045 is a very different claim than it sounds.
SpearmanAntibody traits, rare-earth selectivity Rank-only, and 0.273 is a real but weak signal. Needs cluster-aware confidence intervals to be honest, which they do provide.
Recall at matched precisionBiodefense screening Operationally right — reviewers have finite attention — but depends entirely on the threshold and the composition of the negative set.
Top-k precisionNeoantigen selection, phage prioritization, GWAS Matches the real workflow, hides calibration completely. Their best version converts it into "experiments needed" or "expected hits in 5 slots."
Effect size in natural unitsParkinson's age of onset (years earlier) The most interpretable metric they use, and the hardest to game — but needs the positive and negative controls to be meaningful.
The subtlety most candidates will miss — and it is in their own footnotes

The eQTL panel's labels come from SuSiE statistical fine-mapping of GTEx data: posterior inclusion probability ≥ 0.9 is a positive, ≤ 0.01 a negative. Their own footnote says it: "These labels are statistical inferences from population genetic data, not variants whose effects have been validated by experiment."

So the benchmark measures agreement with another statistical method, not biological truth. A model could improve by better mimicking fine-mapping's biases. Note that their stronger evaluations avoid this: the GWAS prioritization work uses MPRA reporter assays and ADASTRA allele-specific binding — actual measurements — and the E2G benchmark uses CRISPRi perturbations. Raising this distinction unprompted is probably the single highest-signal thing you can say in this interview.

7.2 Holdout design — the principle, and their instances

7.3 Benchmark saturation

The posting asks you to notice "when existing benchmarks stop being informative." Several of theirs are close. Track reconstruction sits at 0.92–0.96 Pearson on easy modalities; MHC class I presentation is 0.939 vs 0.933, a difference nobody should make decisions on. The interesting question is what replaces them, and their own work suggests three answers:

  • Stratify until the metric discriminates again. They slice eQTL by distance and variant class, E2G by number of genes skipped, caQTL by position relative to the peak summit, sQTL by position class. Each time, a slice appears where baselines collapse.
  • Move from inferred labels to experimental ones. MPRA, ADASTRA and CRISPRi sets over fine-mapping-derived labels.
  • Re-express the metric in decision units. "Four experiments instead of five to reach 90% of credible sets." "1.3 of 5 vaccine slots immunogenic instead of 0.7." "−4.4 years age of onset." These are harder to game and immediately interpretable to the scientist on the other side of the table.

Part 9

The dual mandate

Biosecurity is half the stated identity, appears in the job description, and the advisory board is stacked with national-security figures. Expect it to come up; have a real position.

Their formulation: "The same generative breakthroughs that enable life-saving cures also lowers the barrier to creating engineered threats and AI-generated bioweapons. We believe these forces are inseparable. Radical Numerics was founded to develop both the power to design and the responsibility to defend."

What they actually do about it
  • Evo and Evo 2 excluded viruses of eukaryotes from training — a deliberate capability restriction at the dataset level.
  • Omnii Defense ships as a product, with a restricted-access API limited to vetted partners.
  • Edge deployment for environments without reliable connectivity — military installations, rural regions, conflict or disaster zones.
  • The phage post commits to "rigorous precautions and procedures for safe wet-lab research" and to adapting "biosecurity, biosafety, containment, disposal, and data-sharing measures commensurate with advances in Omnii."
  • Lytic rather than temperate phages for therapy, under regulated compassionate use.
  • Engagement with policy bodies: SecureBio's three-day early-warning target, the UK Microbial Forensics Consortium, DTRA, the US Army's edge-sensor prototyping effort.
The tension to be honest about

Evo 2 is "the largest fully open source AI project across any domain," and Nguyen has said openness was deliberate, to drive adoption in a nascent field: "we felt that we had to show people the recipe and make it open source to actually drive adoption."

Omnii-1 is not open. It is an API, a set of partnerships, and a restricted defense product.

That shift is defensible and probably correct given capability growth, but it is a real change in posture from the same team. A thoughtful question about where they now draw the open/closed line, and what evidence would move it, shows you take the mandate seriously rather than treating it as marketing.

Part 11

Interview questions to prepare

How would you post-train a biological foundation model on a modality it has never seen?
Their flagship post-training result · very likely

Anchor on 5hmC: a modality entirely absent from pretraining, learned post hoc, reaching 0.854 Pearson on allele-dosage effects where matched WGS coverage sits at −0.057.

Why it works mechanistically: 5hmC is an epigenetic mark on DNA, so it is already sequence-aligned to the genome coordinate system the model uses for every other track. Attaching it is closer to adding a channel than learning a new domain, and the pretrained model already knows what methylation, accessibility and conservation look like at a locus. The design: a new track embedding and output head, a self-supervised objective, trunk mostly frozen or at low learning rate, and validation on held-out patients rather than held-out positions.

Then raise the research version: what predicts whether a new modality attaches cheaply? Genome-aligned, correlated with existing tracks, continuous-valued is the easy case. A modality with no genomic coordinate — mass spec, imaging, cell-level perturbation screens — is much harder and probably needs a different fusion mechanism. Offering that distinction shows you understand why their architecture choice bought them the result.

Contrast with the antibody case, where the domain was also largely absent from pretraining but required a three-part mixture — general protein to prevent forgetting, public repertoires, partner designs — because the output was generative rather than a track. Two different adaptation shapes, same base model.

This benchmark is saturated. Design the replacement.
Named four separate ways in the job description

Distinguish three reasons a benchmark stops being informative, because they have different fixes: the metric is near ceiling, the labels are not ground truth, or the aggregate hides the slice that matters.

For ceiling effects, stratify until it discriminates — which they already do, slicing eQTL by distance and variant class, E2G by genes skipped, caQTL by position relative to the summit. Point at MHC class I presentation, 0.939 vs 0.933, as a benchmark nobody should be making decisions on.

For label quality, move from inferred to experimental. The eQTL panel's labels come from SuSiE fine-mapping with PIP thresholds, and their own footnote concedes these are "statistical inferences from population genetic data, not variants whose effects have been validated by experiment." Their stronger evaluations already do this — MPRA, ADASTRA, CRISPRi.

For hidden slices, make the holdout unit biological: a clade, a genus, a chromosome, a patient, a sequence cluster.

Close with the move I think is most characteristic of their best work: re-express the metric in decision units. Not AUROC but "four experiments instead of five to reach 90% of credible sets," or "1.3 of 5 vaccine slots immunogenic instead of 0.7." Decision-unit metrics are harder to game, and they force you to state the workflow the model is actually serving.

Your model ranks designs and also generates them. How do you keep that loop honest?
Core risk in their phage, antibody and rare-earth pipelines

Name the failure: when the generator and the scorer are the same model, rejection sampling optimizes toward the model's own confident regions. You get candidates that look excellent to the scorer and may be no better in reality.

The evidence it is live in their own data: on the hard phage strain, the top 100 ranked variants cleared the threshold 24% of the time against a 4.5% base rate. A genuine 5× enrichment — and also three quarters of the model's most confident picks failing.

Mitigations worth proposing, each tied to something they already do:

  • Hold out a slice of every campaign from predictor training, which is exactly how they evaluated phage prioritization — "variants held out from training."
  • Keep a random control arm. The 4.8% random-control recovery rate is what makes 69.9% interpretable. Cheap, and it catches a scorer drifting into self-agreement.
  • Diversity floors in the selected batch, so a round cannot spend its budget on one neighborhood. Note their observation that top-100 lists for different strains shared only 57 members, and were disjoint for the hard strain.
  • Independent scorers where possible — the Omnii+REVEL reciprocal-rank-fusion ensemble is a good precedent for combining a learned model with something that has different biases.
  • Report calibration, not just ranking. Spearman and top-k hide whether predicted fitness is on the right scale.
How does post-training differ when the annotator is a wet lab rather than a human rater?
The conceptual core of the role

Characterize the regime before proposing anything: labels take days to weeks, cost real money, arrive in batches, carry assay and batch noise, and in many cases do not exist at any price. That rules out on-policy RL with frequent environment queries and makes this an offline, active-learning problem where the acquisition decision carries most of the value.

  • Split objectives by label cost. Anything with a cheap in-silico proxy gets enforced at generation time; the expensive budget goes only to what requires measurement.
  • Preference pairs over absolute rewards, holding context fixed — same strain, same assay, same batch. This sidesteps cross-batch calibration, a real problem in assay data.
  • Calibrated uncertainty on the surrogate, because its variance drives acquisition, not just its mean.
  • Multi-objective framing where the objective is a frontier. The antibody work is explicitly about pushing a Pareto front of affinity against expression; scalarizing early destroys information.
  • Cluster-aware everything. With a median 94% identity to nearest neighbor, naive splits and naive confidence intervals are both wrong.

A good closing observation: unlike human preference data, wet-lab labels are objective. A phage forms plaques or it does not. So the usual RLHF worry about noisy subjective raters is replaced by a different one — systematic assay bias, which is correlated rather than random and therefore far more dangerous to a reward model.

Why convolutional operators instead of attention for genomes? Would you keep them?
Their home turf · expect a deep follow-up

The chain: DNA needs single-nucleotide resolution because one base change can abolish function, so you cannot use a chunking tokenizer. That forces sequence lengths of 10⁵–10⁶ tokens for biologically meaningful spans. Quadratic attention is infeasible there. Hyena gives O(L log L) via FFT, with filter length decoupled from parameter count because an MLP generates the filter from positional encodings.

Then the nuance: StripedHyena 2 does not remove attention. It interleaves short-explicit, medium-regularized and long-implicit Hyena operators with multi-head attention. Convolutions are content-agnostic mixers, excellent for spanning distance cheaply, weaker at precise content-based recall. A few attention layers supply what the convolutions lack. The design variable is the ratio and placement — a search problem, and notably the CTO worked on automated architecture design at Liquid AI.

A defensible forward position: the justification is length, so the question is whether cheaper exact attention changes the calculus at 10⁶ tokens. Probably not soon — L² at a million tokens is a different universe from a million-token KV cache in text. The more interesting pressure is that Omnii-1 is multimodal, and track channels may want different mixing than raw sequence does.

How would you study scaling laws for post-training specifically?
Listed as a responsibility; thinly charted in public literature

Note what makes it harder than pretraining. Pretraining has one clean axis pair (parameters, tokens) against one clean metric (validation loss). Post-training has at least four axes the posting names — dataset size, recipe complexity, compute, model family — against a downstream metric that is noisy, bounded, and sometimes saturated.

You can cite a public hint from their own work: in the rare-earth evaluation, scaling ESM-2 from 35M to 15B and ESM-C from 300M to 6B bought only about 0.055 Spearman, and they concluded "model size alone did not determine performance." That is a scaling result that argues for studying recipe rather than size.

  • Fix the base model and sweep post-training dataset size across orders of magnitude, measuring several tasks at once to see whether the curves share a shape.
  • Repeat across model family — the 1B/7B/40B ladder Evo 2 provides — to test whether bigger bases need less post-training data, which is the practically important question.
  • Decompose "recipe complexity" into measurable components (number of stages, number of objectives, curriculum depth) so a negative result is interpretable.
  • Pick metrics with headroom. Fitting a curve against a saturated benchmark produces a flat line that means nothing.
  • Budget for seed variance up front. At small post-training dataset sizes, run-to-run noise can exceed the effect, and reading noise as a trend is the most common failure.

Frame it the way they do: scaling laws exist to decide what to scale next. State the decision the study is meant to inform before you run it.

Where does safety sit in post-training for a model that writes DNA?
Half the company's stated identity

Engage with it as a technical problem rather than a values statement. Post-training is where capability steering happens, so it is the natural control point, and there are real mechanisms: scoping and refusal behavior for certain design requests, data exclusions carried through adaptation (Evo and Evo 2 excluded viruses of eukaryotes at the dataset level), restricted API access for vetted partners, and the fact that the defense product and the design model share a base — screening that improves as generation improves.

The tension to name honestly: a model good enough to design a therapeutic phage is, by construction, good at designing phages. Capability and risk are not separable by an output filter. Their own framing agrees — "these forces are inseparable" — which is why the defensive product is a deliberate hedge rather than a bolt-on.

One concrete contribution a post-training person can make: evaluations for misuse-relevant capability, run with the same rigor as performance evals and tracked across checkpoints, so you know when a capability appeared rather than discovering it after release. That is a direct extension of "build evaluations that actually matter," and it connects to the genus-ablation methodology they already use for generalization.

Pick one of their results and tell us what you would do next.
A likely open-ended closer

Three good candidates, each with a concrete next step:

The antibody one-hot baseline. Omnii scores 0.273 Spearman on binding potency against a one-hot encoding's 0.246, with overlapping intervals. The next experiment is to determine whether the gap is small because the representation is weak or because the dataset is — 327 designs in 172 clusters, all against one target, median 94% identity. I would test on a second target or a more diverse design pool before concluding anything about representation quality, and I would check whether the gap grows with rounds as the campaign explores more sequence space.

The rare-earth structure result. Co-folding Boltz-2 with the bound lanthanide made it worse than using protein representations alone. That is surprising and worth understanding — either the co-folding is inaccurate at this resolution, or selectivity genuinely lives in features outside the coordination sphere, which is what the biology section suggests. Distinguishing those two would tell you whether structure is worth incorporating at all for this class of problem.

The cross-species phage transfer. Adding E. coli data improved P. aeruginosa prediction while hurting the specialist. The natural follow-up is a scaling study: how many species do you need before transfer to a new one becomes reliable, and does it follow a predictable curve? That is directly the "study scaling in post-training" responsibility, on a question they clearly care about.

Part 12

Open problems and honest caveats

Raise some of these yourself. Their own posts are notably forthcoming about limitations, and matching that register is the cheapest way to signal fit with a team that asks you to question whether metrics reflect reality.

Scientific
  • Architecture is undisclosed. No technical report or paper for Omnii-1 — blog posts only. "Proprietary advances in model architecture design" and "multi-scale" is all you get. Do not pretend to know more.
  • n = 1 clinically. One phage patient, outcomes withheld for privacy, and their own statement that "observations from a single patient cannot establish general safety or efficacy."
  • Diagnostics is not prospectively validated. Their words: "Prospective screening studies are still needed to establish clinical benefit."
  • Rare earths is prediction only so far. Generated candidates are in validation; experimental confirmation is explicitly the next step.
  • Antibody correlations are modest and barely clear a one-hot baseline on potency.
  • Rare disease candidates are unconfirmed. The RYR1 splice mechanism "remains to be confirmed experimentally."
  • Some labels are inferred, not measured — the fine-mapping issue.
  • Immunogenicity data is small and heterogeneous, which they state directly.
Strategic
  • Breadth versus depth. Eight applications on launch day is a compelling transfer demo and a lot of surface area for a company this young — each implies partners, regulatory paths, and wet-lab operations.
  • A well-funded incumbent. DeepMind is directly in this space with AlphaGenome, and its licensing already shapes what comparisons are possible.
  • Their own open-source legacy competes with them. Evo 2 is free, capable, and from the same people.
  • The pivot is recent. Recursive self-improvement and general text diffusion in August 2025, general biological intelligence by June 2026. Worth understanding how they think about focus — asked curiously, not skeptically.
  • $50M is a seed-sized budget for frontier-scale multimodal pretraining. Evo 2 alone used 2,048 H100s. Compute strategy is a fair question.
  • Partner-dependent data. Much of the most valuable training signal comes from Aptamino, ClearNote, LLNL and hospital collaborators. That is leverage and also dependency.
